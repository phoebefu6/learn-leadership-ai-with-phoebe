Leadership in the Age of AI · Session 5 of 6

Build, buy, reskill or wait

The bench left the board with a range, not a number: for Marlowe's analysts, somewhere between losing capacity and freeing a fifth of the role, on assumptions nobody has measured yet. A range that wide is still a decision, just a different one. Tonight we split each role by its task mix into four options, reskill the people around the tasks a chat model helps with today, build or buy the software the other exposed tasks need, or wait and measure, and we put a cost and a payback on each, as bands. Then we price the option nobody puts on a slide: waiting a quarter on purpose.

Part 0

Where we start

Marlowe Group is constructed: an invented 4,000-person professional-services firm whose chief executive, Hana Reyes, owes her board a workforce plan for AI. In session 4 the bench walked the management analysts, 900 people in the advisory practice (constructed headcount), from "82 percent exposed" down to a board range on the real O*NET task rows. Hana's board now asks the obvious next question: so what do we spend, on what, and when? Every Marlowe number is invented for teaching; the task data and the bench's printed values underneath them are real.

Part 1 · covers the four options and the task mix that picks them

Four options, chosen task by task

5 min live

The exposure rubric from session 1 already contains the buying decision. An E1 task is one a language model in a chat window could halve today; the saving needs people who use the tool well, not software. An E2 task is one only software built on top of the model could halve; that software has to be built or bought before anyone saves an hour. An E0 task is left as it is. The four options follow from the labels, one task group at a time.

LiveWhy the option belongs to the task, not the role3 min▶

A board paper that says "we will reskill the analysts" or "we will buy a platform for the analysts" has chosen one option for eleven different tasks. On the human labels, two analyst tasks are E1: designing changes to forms and reports, and planning studies of work problems. A chat model helps there now, so the spend is on people: training, practice, and time for seniors to check what juniors produce. Seven tasks are E2. Training alone saves nothing on them, because the saving only exists once software built on top of the model is in place. Two are E0 and need no option at all.

So a constructed Marlowe memo for the analysts reads as three lines, not one: reskill around two tasks now, build or buy for seven, leave two alone. And it carries a fourth line for the role as a whole, because the bench showed the case turns on review time and new demand, which brings in waiting. The general build-against-buy frameworks are taught in Strategic thinking ↗; here the only question is which tasks each option is for.

Self-studyBuild or buy: the E2 half-weight is an investment3 min▶

Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin and Rock count an E2 task at half weight in their beta measure, and they give the reason: the half stands for the extra investment the software needs. That is the build-or-buy decision written as a coefficient. The bench inherits it as an assumption: an E2 task saves half of what an E1 task saves, 12.5 percent against 25 percent on the defaults, and on those defaults it costs 10 percent of its original time to check. Review eats most of the E2 saving before any software has been paid for.

That gives the split a test. Build where the E2 task is specific to the firm, its methods, its data, its clients' formats, and where you can own the checking step. Buy where the task is common across firms, so a vendor has already paid for the software and spreads it across customers. In both cases the authors' caution applies: exposure "does not necessarily suggest that their tasks can be fully automated", and real time savings would likely be "slightly or significantly lower" than their estimates. A vendor demo is evidence about a vendor demo.

Real world

Building and buying create tasks as well as removing them. Humlum and Vestergaard (NBER Working Paper 33777, revised 2026) linked Danish adoption surveys to administrative records and found precise null effects on earnings and recorded hours, ruling out effects larger than 2 percent two years after ChatGPT's launch. What they did find was reorganisation: employers absorbing AI through new tasks in content generation, AI oversight and AI integration. Whichever of build or buy Marlowe picks, someone inherits the oversight and integration work, and the memo should name who.

Part 2 · covers reskill cost and payback as ranges

What reskilling costs, in FTE-years, as a band

5 min live

The bench prices reskilling without a single salary. Its cost is a modelled band of 0.03 to 0.08 FTE-years per person in the role, a share of one person's loaded year covering training time and programme cost, and its payback is that band divided by the FTE the role frees each year. Both ends of both numbers are assumptions, and the board should see them as such.

LiveReading cost and payback off the bench3 min▶

For the analysts on the default assumptions, the bench's board rung prints a freed range of -48.0 to 177.4 FTE and a reskilling payback of 2 months to never. The fast end pairs the cheap end of the cost band with the fast case: 50 percent saved on E1 tasks, 5 percent review, nothing absorbed by new demand. The slow end pairs the expensive end with the slow case: 14 percent saved, 15 percent review, half absorbed. In the slow case the analysts' review costs more than the tool saves, the freed capacity is negative, and a payback cannot be computed, so the bench says "never" rather than inventing a number.

Hana's board line for the analysts, in the constructed Marlowe case, is therefore a sentence with two ends and a condition: "Reskilling the analysts pays back in as little as 2 months if the tool is fast and checking is light; it never pays back if checking takes 15 percent of the task. We have not measured checking." Your numbers will differ the moment you move an assumption, and a reader can see which one to move.

Self-studyWhat the band covers, and why it is in FTE-years3 min▶

The band of 0.03 to 0.08 FTE-years per person is modelled and constructed; no study supplies it. It covers the hours people spend training instead of working and the cost of running the programme, expressed as a share of one loaded working year so that the plan never shows a salary. Multiply it by the role's headcount and you have the role's cost band in FTE-years, which compares directly with the FTE the role frees. Replace it with your own finance team's band before anything goes to the board.

Two things it does not cover. It does not price build or buy: software cost belongs in the same memo, in the same unit if finance can express it that way, and as its own band. And it does not price the senior time that checking takes, which the bench already subtracts as review load. Adding it again would count it twice; leaving review out of the bench would hide it. How the change programme itself is run, adoption and culture, is in AI leadership ↗.

Real world

A payback computed on the forecast speed-up can point the wrong way. Becker, Rush, Barnes and Rein at METR ran a randomised study with 16 experienced open-source developers on 246 tasks in their own repositories. The developers forecast that AI would cut their completion time by 24 percent; afterwards they believed it had cut it by 20 percent; measured, it made them 19 percent slower. A payback built on what people expected would have been positive; the measured one, on that task, would have been never. It is one task and one group, not a verdict on every role, which is exactly why the slow case stays in the range.

Part 3 · covers the option value of waiting and the macro range

Waiting is a decision with a price

5 min live

The range for the analysts crosses zero because two inputs are assumptions: how long checking takes, and how much of the freed time new work absorbs. No study measures either at firm scale. Both can be measured inside Marlowe, on one team, in one quarter. Waiting is the option that buys that measurement, and it has a price that belongs in the memo next to the other three.

LiveWhat a quarter of measurement buys3 min▶

Waiting well is not doing nothing. Hana's constructed plan gives one analyst team the tool for the E1 tasks, has its seniors log the hours they spend checking output, and tracks how much of the time freed goes to new work: more proposals, deeper analysis, work previously turned away. At the end of the quarter the bench's two pure assumptions, review at 10 percent and demand at 25 percent, are replaced by Marlowe's own figures, and the range narrows to whatever those figures say.

The price is real and should be written down: the fast case's freed hours that do not arrive for a quarter, and E2 software that starts a quarter later. What it buys is protection against the anti-lever from session 4, cutting the team on the time-share hours and then finding that the checking needs most of them back. Waiting has the most value when the range crosses zero, as it does for the analysts, and the least when even the slow case frees capacity. The tripwires that end the wait, and who reads them, are session 6.

Self-studyThe macro story has a low side and a high side3 min▶

Boards hear both, often in the same week. On the low side, Acemoglu's "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" (Economic Policy, 2025) estimates the total factor productivity gain over ten years at no more than 0.66 percent, and less than 0.53 percent once hard-to-learn tasks are accounted for; the gains depend on the fraction of tasks impacted and the average cost saving per task. He also predicts AI widens the gap between capital and labour income. On the other side, employers surveyed by the World Economic Forum expect a large reshuffle of jobs and skills by 2030 (reported).

These disagree, and the disagreement does not settle a single role at a single firm. A model of the whole economy can sit low while one firm's E1-heavy roles change fast; employer expectations can run high while measured earnings and hours barely move, as Humlum and Vestergaard found in Denmark. Use them as context for the range, say which is a model and which is a survey, and decide on what you measure. The theory underneath, displacement and reinstatement, is in Economics, session 7 ↗.

Real world

Employers expect churn, and they said so in a survey. The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 (reported; the WEF pages could not be read directly) records employers expecting about 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net 78 million, with about 39 percent of core skills expected to change. It is a survey of what employers expect, not a measurement of what happened. For a reskilling budget it is a reason to expect skills to move; it is not a payback figure for any role.

Build-along

A one-page option memo per role

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Hana asks for one page per role, in the same six lines every time, so the board can read twenty roles in an evening. You will write it for the role you inventoried in session 1 and ran on the bench in session 4, or for Marlowe's management analysts. Marlowe's version is the worked example in every step; its numbers are the bench's printed values and constructed choices, and your numbers will differ.

Write the role and its task mix, with the rater. Take the counts from your session 1 inventory: how many tasks are E1, E2 and E0, on whose labels. Add the headcount and the share in the most junior grade, because session 3 showed where the exposed work tends to sit.

★ Step 1 · role and task mixRole: Management analyst, advisory practice Headcount: 900 (Marlowe, constructed); junior grade 405 Task mix: 2 E1, 7 E2, 2 E0 (Eloundou human labels, O*NET 31.0) Contested: "Prepare manuals and train workers" is E2 to the human raters and E0 to GPT-4

Assign an option to each task group. E1 tasks get reskill. E2 tasks get build or buy, decided task by task: build where the task is specific to your firm, buy where the market already serves it. E0 tasks get nothing. Write one reason per line.

★ Step 2 · option per task groupE1 (2 tasks) RESKILL plan studies, design form changes; a chat model helps today E2 (7 tasks) BUILD analyse data, document findings: Marlowe's own method and templates BUY gather and organise information: common to every advisory firm E0 (2 tasks) KEEP interview staff, confer on new systems (choices constructed for Marlowe; your split will differ)

Put the cost in FTE-years, as a band. Use the bench's reskill band unless finance gives you your own, and multiply by headcount. Give build and buy their own band, in the same unit if finance can express it that way. No salaries on the page.

★ Step 3 · cost bandReskill: 0.03 to 0.08 FTE-years per person (bench assumption, modelled) x 900 people; read the role total off the bench Build/buy: a separate band from finance and the vendor shortlist, in FTE-years; not yet priced Not here: checking time; the bench already subtracts it as review

Copy the range and the payback from the bench, both ends. Rung 5, as printed. If the slow end says "never", write "never" and say which assumption makes it so.

★ Step 4 · range and paybackFreed, rung 5: -48.0 to 177.4 FTE of 900 (modelled range) Payback: 2 months (fast case) to never (slow case) Why "never": at 14% saved and 15% review, checking costs more than the tool saves Default rung 4: 26.1 FTE, on review 10% and demand 25% (assumptions)

Write what you will measure while you wait, and on whom. The two pure assumptions first: review hours per exposed task and the share of freed time new work absorbs. One team, one quarter, logged by the people doing the checking.

★ Step 5 · wait and measurePilot: one analyst team, the 2 E1 tasks, one quarter Measure: senior hours spent checking AI-assisted output, per task share of freed hours that went to new client work junior intake held as planned (the session 3 rung) Owner: head of the advisory practice Cost of waiting: the fast case's hours, deferred one quarter

Write the decision and the trigger in one sentence. Name what you commit now, what you hold, and the measured number that releases it. This is the line session 6 turns into a decision record with tripwires.

★ Step 6 · decision and triggerManagement analyst: reskill around the 2 E1 tasks now; hold the E2 build and buy for one quarter; commit them once the measured slow case, on our own review and demand figures, is above zero. Range today: -48.0 to 177.4 FTE; payback 2 months to never.

One memo per role, the same six lines every time. The board compares roles by the decision line and the range; anything that does not fit on the page goes in an appendix. Hiring new people into a redesigned role is a separate discipline, taught in AI in HR ↗; if a memo ever leads to a smaller team, parting well is in Performance management ↗.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On the human labels the management analysts have 2 E1 tasks, 7 E2 tasks and 2 E0 tasks. Which plan fits that mix?

The option follows the label. Training helps where a chat model already halves the time; on E2 tasks the saving needs software, which is why Eloundou et al. count them at half weight for the extra investment. A spends on people where software is missing, C spends on software where two tasks need none, and D turns a task count into headcount, the step session 2 took apart.

2 · The bench prints a reskilling payback of 2 months to never for the analysts. What should the board paper say?

A range is the only honest form, and "never" has no midpoint to average. The slow end exists because review at 15 percent outweighs a 14 percent saving; naming that assumption tells the board what to measure. A vendor cannot confirm the firm's own review time.

3 · Acemoglu's model puts the ten-year productivity gain at no more than 0.66 percent, while employers surveyed by the WEF expect a large reshuffle of skills (reported). How should a board use the two?

They measure different things, an economy-wide model and a survey of expectations, and they disagree. Neither sizes one role at one firm. Picking one hides the disagreement and averaging invents a number neither source gives; the quarter of measurement is what turns context into a decision.
