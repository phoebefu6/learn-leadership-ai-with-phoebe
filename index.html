Learn Leadership in the Age of AI with Phoebe

Organising for AI, for the executives who decide a company-wide workforce strategy. AI changes tasks, not job titles, so the organisation has to be redesigned from the task up: what each role really consists of, where the hours a tool frees actually go, what happens to layers and to the junior rung that feeds the senior ones, and how to put a range rather than a promise in front of a board. One constructed running case, Marlowe Group, a 4,000-person professional-services firm, and one bench: 34 real occupations and 624 O*NET task statements with published exposure labels, computed in your browser.

The six sessions 🧭 · from the task list to the board's decision record

The running case is Marlowe Group, a constructed professional-services firm of 4,000 consultants, accountants, analysts, lawyers, technologists and operations staff. Its board has asked for a workforce plan for AI, and its chief executive wants one she can defend line by line. Marlowe, its people and every Marlowe number are invented for teaching; the task data under them is real. Workload here means planning, never medical or clinical advice.

How large the productivity gain isContested. Field studies find gains of about 14 to 25 percent on some tasks, one trial found experienced developers 19 percent slower, and a macro estimate puts the decade's total factor productivity gain under 1 percent. Taught as the disagreement
What AI is doing to early-career jobsContested. Payroll data show early-career employment in exposed occupations below trend, described by its authors as descriptive, not causal; Danish records show no effect on earnings or hours yet
