Leadership in the Age of AI · Session 6 of 6

Governing the transition

Five sessions produced a memo per role: a task mix, a range that for Marlowe's analysts runs from losing capacity to freeing a fifth of the role, and a decision held until two numbers are measured. A board cannot approve a range and walk away. What it can do is record the decision with its assumptions in plain sight, fix in advance the measured numbers that reopen it, and look at those numbers every quarter. Tonight we write that record, set two tripwires, on review load and on the junior rung, and decide what to re-measure once the change is under way.

Part 0

Where we start

The running case is constructed. Marlowe Group is an invented professional-services firm of 4,000 people, and its chief executive, Hana Reyes, now takes the option memos from session 5 to the board. The board does not want another forecast. It wants to know what it is agreeing to, what it is not yet agreeing to, and what would make it change its mind. Marlowe, Hana and every Marlowe number are invented for teaching; the evidence underneath them is real and cited.

Part 1 · covers the board-level decision record

A decision the board can reopen

5 min live

At Marlowe (constructed), the analysts' memo says: reskill around the two directly exposed tasks now, hold the software spend for a quarter, commit it once the measured slow case is above zero. That is a good decision and a fragile one, because two of the numbers behind it are assumptions nobody has measured. A decision record keeps the decision and its weak points on the same page, so the board that signs it knows exactly which lines a quarter of evidence could overturn.

LiveWhy the record carries the assumptions, not only the decision3 min▶

The exposure labels underneath every memo describe potential, and their authors say so. Eloundou and colleagues write that exposure "does not necessarily suggest that their tasks can be fully automated", that real-world time savings would likely be "slightly or significantly lower" than their estimates, and they make no prediction about adoption timelines. Two of the bench's inputs have no firm-level estimate at all: review time per exposed task, and the share of freed time that new work absorbs.

So a board decision built on them is a decision under named uncertainty. The professional norm is to write the uncertainty down next to the decision, with the date it will be measured, rather than bury it in an appendix. A record that says "review 10 percent, not measured yet" is one a successor board can audit; a record that says "26 analysts freed" is one it can only believe or disbelieve.

Self-studySeven lines, and what each one is for4 min▶

Decision says what is committed and what is held, in one sentence. Evidence and rater carries the task counts with the name of who labelled them, because session 1 showed two careful raters disagree on 108 of Marlowe's 624 tasks. Range copies both ends from the bench as printed, slow case and fast case, never the midpoint alone. Assumptions lists every input that is a stated rule rather than a measurement.

Named for counsel records that legal duties exist and who is checking them, without the board pretending to know the answer. Tripwires fix the numbers that reopen the decision. Owner and review date make one person answerable for reading the wires and put the next look in the diary. Seven lines fit on a page; a record that needs two pages is usually carrying an argument that belongs in the memo.

Real world

A familiar pattern: the plan that outlives its assumptions. A restructuring is approved on a business case with a productivity figure in it. A year later the figure has quietly become a target, the people who made the estimate have moved on, and nobody can say whether it was ever measured. The fix is not a better estimate. It is writing, on the day of approval, which figure is assumed, who will measure it, and what the board will do if the measurement comes in low.

Part 2 · covers tripwires on review load and the junior rung

Two tripwires, set before the results come in

5 min live

A tripwire is a measured number, fixed in advance, that reopens a decision when it is crossed. Fixing it before the results arrive is the point: once a programme is running, every number can be explained away. Marlowe's record carries two (constructed thresholds), one on each place the bench showed a plan could quietly fail: the checking load that eats the saving, and the junior rung that a cut on hours empties.

LiveThe review-load wire, and where 12.5 percent comes from3 min▶

On the bench's default assumptions an E2 task saves 12.5 percent of its time and costs 10 percent to check. The margin is two and a half points. If checking turns out to take 12.5 percent, every E2 task the software touches frees nothing; above it, the firm loses time. That is why Marlowe's wire sits there, and why the analysts' range has a negative slow end: at the bottom of the assumed savings and the top of the assumed review, checking costs more than the tool saves and payback prints "never".

The bench's break button shows how much rides on this one input: with review set to zero, the analysts free 80.0 FTE at rung 4 instead of 26.1. The difference is the checking. No study measures review time at firm scale, so the wire has to be read from the firm's own logs: hours spent checking AI-assisted output, per task, recorded by the people doing the checking. Measured that way it is a number; asked about at a town hall it is a feeling, and the METR study below shows how far those two can sit apart.

LiveThe junior-rung wire, without settling the argument3 min▶

The evidence on junior workers points two ways, and the record should say so. Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond found that an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 34 percent for novice and low-skilled support agents, against 14 percent on average and minimal impact for the most experienced: on that reading, assistants help juniors most. Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen's "Canaries in the Coal Mine?", revised in August 2026 on payroll data through June 2026, find employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations 19 percent below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers, with no comparable gap for experienced workers.

The canaries authors call their facts descriptive, not causal; some trends predate generative AI, and the gap weakens when education is controlled for. Both findings can be partly true. The wire does not need to choose between them. It watches the firm's own number: on the bench's anti-lever case, a cut sized on the rung 2 hours removes 106 analysts and takes the junior grade from 405 to 299, because exposed tasks skew to entry-level work. Any unplanned drop below the plan brings the decision back to the board, where a deliberate choice can be made in the open.

Self-studySetting a wire that works3 min▶

Four properties separate a tripwire from a target. It is fixed before the first result, so nobody tunes it to the data. It is measured, from a log or a headcount record, not from a survey of how people feel. It has one owner who reports it whether or not it has tripped. And its response is agreed in advance: a tripped wire reopens the decision at the next board; it does not trigger an automatic cut, hire or spend.

Two wires are enough for one role. A record with ten wires gets read like a dashboard and ignored like one. Pick the inputs the bench showed the decision is most sensitive to, which for most exposed roles are review load and the junior rung, and leave the rest to the quarterly re-measure in Part 3.

Real world

Experienced developers felt faster and were slower. Becker, Rush, Barnes and Rein at METR ran 16 experienced open-source developers through 246 tasks in their own repositories. Before the study the developers forecast AI would cut completion time by 24 percent; afterwards they believed it had cut it by 20 percent; measured, AI made them 19 percent slower. One study, one kind of work and early-2025 tools, so it does not set the size of the effect elsewhere. What it settles for governance is narrower: self-reported speed is not a measurement, and a wire built on it can stay green while the time goes the other way.

Part 3 · covers what to measure after, and what the record hands on

Measure the tasks, not only the payroll

5 min live

The tempting after-measure is the one already on the finance pack: headcount, hours, cost per role. The best evidence so far says that is the slowest place the change appears. At Marlowe (constructed), Hana's quarterly review re-measures the four inputs the bench depends on, reruns it on the firm's own figures, and only then looks at payroll.

LiveWhy payroll can stay flat while the work changes3 min▶

Humlum and Vestergaard linked Danish adoption surveys to administrative records and found precise null effects on earnings and recorded hours two years after ChatGPT's launch, ruling out effects larger than 2 percent. What they did find is employers absorbing AI through task reorganisation, including new tasks: content generation, AI oversight, AI integration. The jobs kept their titles and their pay; the contents moved.

For a board that means the payroll line is a lagging and possibly silent indicator. The four measures in the figure are leading ones, and each feeds a bench input directly: re-surveyed time shares replace the importance-times-frequency proxy, logged review hours replace the 10 percent assumption, the absorbed share replaces the 25 percent one, and junior intake checks the wire. New tasks belong in the re-survey too: an "AI oversight" task that did not exist last year is part of the review load, and should be counted as such. The labour economics behind these findings sits in Economics, session 7 ↗.

LiveWhat the record names and hands to others3 min▶

Changing roles at scale can bring legal duties to inform and consult employees or their representatives, including works councils, and their scope and timing differ by country. The record names that these duties exist, says who is taking advice on them and by when, and stops there; what they require is a question for employment counsel in each jurisdiction, not for a board paper.

Three other questions the transition raises have owners of their own, and the record points to them rather than answering them. Culture, change adoption and the ethics of how AI is introduced: AI leadership ↗. Parting well, if a redesigned role ever means fewer people: Performance management ↗. Adverse impact, if the redesign changes who is hired or selected: AI in HR ↗.

Self-studyRerunning the bench on your own figures3 min▶

The bench was built to be rerun. On its defaults, Marlowe's 34 roles free 128.7 FTE of 3,660 at rung 4 on the human labels and 135.5 on GPT-4's, with a firm-wide range of -188.9 to 797.8 FTE. With review set to zero the rung 4 figure is 356.0: the checking assumption alone moves the firm's answer by more than two hundred people. Those are the numbers a first quarter of measurement starts to replace.

Read the rerun the same way every quarter: both ends of rung 5 first, then which input moved and why. A narrowing range is progress even when the midpoint does not change, because it means an assumption has become a measurement. A range that still crosses zero after two quarters is information too: the held decision stays held, and the record says so rather than drifting into a commitment by default.

Real world

Denmark: the change showed up in tasks first. Humlum and Vestergaard's "Still Waters, Rapid Currents" (NBER Working Paper 33777, May 2025, revised March 2026) links surveys of AI adoption to Danish administrative records. Two years after ChatGPT's launch they find precise nulls on earnings and recorded hours, ruling out effects larger than 2 percent, alongside task reorganisation and new tasks in content generation, AI oversight and AI integration. It sits beside other evidence that disagrees on the size and timing of job effects; what it gives a board is a reason to look inside the job before looking at the payroll.

Build-along

The decision record and a quarterly agenda

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Take one option memo from session 5, for a role at your own organisation or for Marlowe's management analysts, and turn it into the seven-line record. Then write the agenda for the first quarterly review, in the order the board should read it: wires first, everything else after. Marlowe's version is the worked example; your numbers will differ.

Copy the decision line from the option memo. One sentence: what is committed now, what is held, and the measured number that releases the hold. If it takes two sentences, the decision is two decisions; write two records.

★ Step 1 · decisionRole: Management analyst, advisory practice 900 people (Marlowe, constructed); junior grade 405 Decision: reskill around the 2 E1 tasks now; hold the E2 build and buy for one quarter; commit them once the measured slow case, on our own review and demand figures, is above zero

Write the evidence with its rater, and the range as printed. Task counts with the name of who labelled them; both ends of rung 5 and the payback, from the bench. No midpoint on its own.

★ Step 2 · evidence and rangeEvidence: 2 E1, 7 E2, 2 E0 of 11 O*NET 31.0 tasks (human labels, Eloundou et al.); 1 task flips to not exposed on GPT-4's labels Range: -48.0 to 177.4 FTE of 900 (rung 5, modelled) Payback: 2 months (fast case) to never (slow case)

List every assumption and the date it gets measured. Anything on the bench that is a stated rule rather than a measurement goes here, with who measures it.

★ Step 3 · assumptionsReview per exposed task: 10% (assumption) measured from Q1 checking logs Freed time absorbed: 25% (assumption) measured from Q1 client-hours split Time shares: importance x frequency proxy replaced by the re-survey in Q1 Owner of all three: head of the advisory practice

Set two tripwires, each with a threshold, a source and a response. Fix the numbers now. The response is always "reopen at the next board", never an automatic action.

★ Step 4 · tripwiresWire 1: review on E2 tasks reaches 12.5% of the task's original time (source: checking logs, per task) Wire 2: junior grade falls below 405 without a board decision (source: monthly headcount record) If either trips: the decision reopens at the next board; nothing is cut, hired or bought automatically (thresholds constructed for Marlowe; yours will differ)

Name what the board is not deciding, and who is. Legal duties go to counsel with a date. Adoption, exits and hiring go to their owners with a link, not a paragraph.

★ Step 5 · named and handed onCounsel: information and consultation duties in each country with analysts; advice due before Q1 review Adoption and culture: chief people officer Exits, if any: chief people officer, separate paper Adverse impact: head of talent acquisition (owners constructed for Marlowe)

Write the agenda for the first quarterly review. Thirty minutes, wires first. Put the output at the bottom so everyone knows what the meeting has to produce.

★ Step 6 · quarterly agenda0-5 Wires: E2 review vs 12.5%; junior grade vs 405 5-15 Measured vs assumed: time shares, review hours, absorbed share 15-20 Bench rerun, rungs 4 and 5, both ends as printed 20-25 Decide: release, hold or reverse the E2 spend 25-30 Hand-offs: counsel, adoption, exits, hiring Output: record re-signed, next review date set

One record per role, the same seven lines every time. A board reads ten records faster than one long paper, because it can compare the tripwire lines side by side. Keep the option memo as the appendix; the record is what gets signed.

Homework

Before your first quarterly review

30 min
  1. Finish the record for your role: all seven lines, with a rater on the evidence and a measurement date on every assumption.
  2. Find the source for each wire. Confirm the checking log and the headcount record exist and that someone can pull the number by the review date. A wire with no source is a hope.
  3. Book the counsel conversation. Write down which countries the role sits in and who is taking advice on consultation duties, with a date before the first review.
  4. Diarise the review. Put the 30-minute agenda in the board calendar for next quarter, with the wire owner named as first speaker.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · In the METR study, developers believed AI had made them 20 percent faster; measured, it made them 19 percent slower. What does that mean for the review-load tripwire?

The gap between belief and measurement is the lesson: a wire built on perception could stay green while time goes the other way. A overreads one study of one kind of work. B is exactly the measure METR shows can mislead. D keeps an assumption the record exists to replace.

2 · Early-career employment in the most AI-exposed occupations is 19 percent below trend in the canaries study. What should the board record do with that?

The authors call the facts descriptive, not causal, and the novice gain comes from a different setting; both can be partly true. A and B each pretend one side has won. C adds two different measures together. The wire watches the firm's own number without having to settle the argument.

3 · Two quarters in, Marlowe's payroll shows no change in earnings or recorded hours. Has the transition done nothing?

Humlum and Vestergaard found precise nulls on earnings and hours two years after ChatGPT's launch while tasks inside jobs were reorganised. Flat payroll is consistent with real change in the work. B and C read the lagging line as the verdict; D gets the order the wrong way round.
